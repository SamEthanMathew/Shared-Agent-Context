Sharing

Share a project brain like you'd share a document.

Three independent controls, in the shape you already know from Google Docs. Change one and see who can read the context.

People

Invite by email at view, edit, or manage. If they don't have an account yet, signing up through the invite joins them.

Anyone with the link

For a contractor or a reviewer you don't want to chase for an account. Generating a new link invalidates every copy already circulating.

Organisation

When several contexts belong to the same group of people, grant the whole group at once instead of person by person.

Who can read this context

    An assistant can never share

    Granting access is a human action, taken in the app. There is nothing an assistant can do to let someone else in — not by mistake, and not by being talked into it.

    Nothing opens by surprise

    Putting a context into an organisation grants nobody anything until you say so. Grouping projects and exposing them are two separate decisions.

    Turning it off doesn't evict people

    Switching link sharing off stops new people joining. Anyone already inside keeps the access you gave them until you remove it.

    Privacy and control

    Shared deliberately. Traceable by default.

    Connecting an assistant does not mean handing over everything. Osmos decides what each person may read and write — the model never gets to make that call.

    Private stays private

    Your teammates' private notes never reach the model at all. Not removed from the answer afterwards — never sent in the first place.

    Nothing goes to a third party

    Your project memory is never sent anywhere else to make Osmos work. No outside model reads it, summarises it, or indexes it: finding what matters happens in the same database your memory already lives in, and nowhere else.

    Disconnect any assistant

    Every assistant you connect is listed, and you can cut any one of them off on its own. The next thing it tries to read fails.

    Where it came from

    Who said it, which assistant published it, what it was based on, and when — attached to the knowledge itself, not reconstructed later.

    Look at any of it

    Read what the project believes, see who changed access and when, and retract anything that shouldn't be there.

    Nothing is deleted behind you

    Archiving a project hides it from every assistant immediately and keeps the memory. Nothing is removed for a billing reason either.

    Questions

    Is this another chat app?

    No. There is no Osmos chat interface and nothing to migrate into. It runs underneath the assistants you already use and gives them a shared project memory.

    Does Osmos read my whole ChatGPT or Claude history?

    No. It receives what your assistant deliberately sends it — what it is working on, and the durable knowledge worth keeping. Transcripts are not uploaded. The point is to share decisions and findings, not conversations.

    What do my teammates actually see?

    Only what was shared to a context they have access to. Your private notes are yours alone: their assistant is never sent them, so keeping them private does not depend on a model choosing to keep a secret.

    Can I keep client work separate from everything else?

    Yes — that's what contexts are for. Each one has its own memory, members, and access, and an agent works in exactly one at a time. You can also pin a context to a single conversation so switching in one chat doesn't move the others.

    How do I bring in someone who doesn't have an account?

    Invite their email address and they get a link; signing up through it joins them. Or turn on "anyone with the link" for view or edit access when an account isn't worth the friction. A link can never grant the right to re-share.

    Which assistants work today?

    Claude and ChatGPT, added as a connector in each. Anything else that speaks the same connector standard works too, and there is an API for everything that doesn't. The rest are listed as planned above, and we won't call one available until it's documented and tested.

    Is my project memory used to train anything?

    No. Osmos never sends your memory to any third party — there is no embedding model, no summarisation model, and no outside call involved in finding what's relevant. That work happens where your memory already is, and nothing about it is used to train anything.

    What does it cost, and how long is data kept?

    Free covers one context with a few people and assistants; Pro is $8 per person per month. See pricing. Your memory and the record of who shared what are kept for as long as the context exists. The records of what each assistant was shown expire after 90 days, because each one lists a person's private notes and shouldn't become an archive in its own right.

    What happens when a decision changes?

    The new decision explicitly replaces the old one. The old version stays in the history, with who made it and when, and assistants stop being told the version you abandoned.

    What if a project ends?

    Archive the context. It leaves every list and assistants stop seeing it immediately, but nothing is deleted — restoring it brings all of the memory back exactly as it was.
